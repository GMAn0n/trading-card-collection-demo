How scanning works here

  1. 1Turn on the camera and allow access when your browser asks — on a phone it uses the rear camera automatically. Rather not use the live view? Upload a photo instead: on a phone the picker offers your camera or your library.
  2. 2Fill the card-shaped guide with the card, edge to edge — including its bottom edge, where the collector number sits — in good light, and tap Take photo. The preview shows the camera's full field (nothing is cropped away) and the photo is kept at full resolution. A graded card can stay in its case — photograph the whole slab and the label is read too.
  3. 3Let it identify the card. First, AI reads the photo (sent to our card-data proxy) and returns the card's name, HP, attacks, Pokémon Power / Ability name, collector number, illustrator, finish (holo, reverse holo or non-holo), 1st Edition stamp, set symbol and © year line — and, when the card sits in a grading slab, the label's company, grade and cert number. The read also reports the card's language and its name exactly as printed: printings in other languages are different cards with their own values, so a non-English read is matched only within that language's printings — a Japanese card is never swapped for the English printing. If that can't read it, the on-device fallback takes over: it finds the card's edges in the photo, squares it up, and reads the name in the top band (HP beside it as a cross-check) and the collector number in the bottom-right. Either way, every printing of that name is then scored on the combination — number, HP, each attack (name first, damage only as a bonus), the Pokémon Power name, the illustrator, the finish, the edition stamp, the set symbol and the © years — which is what separates look-alike reprints like Base Set from Base Set 2, since they share name, HP and attacks, and near-twins like the GB-promo Dragonite from the Fossil one, which share species, HP and an attack but differ in power and illustrator. The Pokédex number on a Japanese card's data strip counts only as weak supporting evidence — it's the species number, not a unique card id, so it never decides a match on its own. Illustrator and finish reads are noisy, so they only ever add points when they agree — a mismatch never counts against a printing. A read number that no printing of that name carries is treated as a misread and ignored, never shown as fact. A printing opens automatically only on a clear win — a strong score with a clear margin, or at least three independent signal types agreeing; name and HP alone never auto-pick. Anything close goes to a picker where each printing lists the signals that matched it, so your tap is an informed one. If nothing reads clearly, it says so and asks for a retake instead of guessing. The headline value is the raw (ungraded) price for that printing from PriceCharting, with TCGplayer market prices per finish alongside and live eBay listings. Identity is cross-checked across multiple card databases and prices come from a separate pricing source, which is why a printing missing from one catalogue can still be identified and valued.
  4. 4Add it in one tap. From a photo, the card page leads with a one-tap Add to collection — Raw by default, with Damaged and Graded (PSA / CGC / BGS / SGC / TAG) right there on the same sheet. If the photo showed a grading slab, that sheet opens with Graded already selected — company and grade filled in from the label, cert number included — and the copy is valued from that grader's ladder row. A photo can't prove a 1st Edition stamp, so photo adds default to Unlimited unless you switch.

About · Data & Partners

What this demo does, where its data comes from, and how buying works.

What it is

A trading card companion: photograph or search any Pokémon or Magic card, see what it's worth right now, track a collection's total value, and keep a wishlist with target prices — all in one place, on phone or desktop.

Data sources

Headline values are the raw (ungraded) price for the exact printing — name, set and collector number — from PriceCharting, fetched per card: a Base Set Raichu, a Fossil Raichu and a 1st Edition printing each carry their own value. Pokémon card images, sets and TCGplayer market prices come live from TCGdex, with the Pokémon TCG API as backup; those TCGplayer figures are near-mint market prices, shown as labelled per-finish rows (1st Edition and Unlimited separately), never as the headline. That TCGplayer pricing is the same catalogue TCGCSV mirrors as daily price files. Magic data and prices come from Scryfall. Prices are fetched fresh each time you search.

Buying

Every card page shows two ways to buy that exact printing. For sale on TCGplayer: one product card per finish (Holofoil, Normal, Reverse Holofoil…) with the TCGplayer product photo, “from” lowest current ask, market price and TCGplayer Direct price when available — filter by finish and sort by lowest ask or market price, then tap through to that finish's TCGplayer product page with every seller. Live on eBay: thumbnail, price + shipping and all-in total, bids and end time on auctions, seller and condition — fetched live via the eBay search proxy, with filters for Auction / Buy It Now and sorting by best match, price, or ending soonest. Purchases happen on the marketplace — this demo never handles payment.

For partners

Built by Muse Enterprise Solutions as a working product exhibit for marketplace and data partnership conversations — including TCGplayer affiliate (Impact) and partner data access. Photo identification (an AI read of the printed name, HP, attacks, collector number, illustrator, finish, edition stamp, set symbol and © year line — plus grading-slab labels: company, grade and cert — scored across every printing of the name, with an on-device OCR fallback) and graded-card states are live in this demo; the full version adds population data and daily price alerts.
